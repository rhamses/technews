While it can work and pilots are trained for it, the risk/reward ratio is far from favorable and it should always be a last resort situation.</p>\n<p><span><img alt=\"cockpit.png\" src=\"https://clickhouse.com/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Fcockpit_5ce331be63.png&w=2048&q=75\"></span></p>\n<p>As the initial <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/engineering-resources/best-open-source-observability-solutions\">pillar of observability</a>, centralized logging can be used for a wide variety of purposes: from debugging production incidents, resolving customer support tickets, mitigating security breaches, and even understanding how customers use the product.</p>\n<p>The most important feature of any centralized log store is its ability to quickly aggregate, analyze, and search through vast amounts of log data from diverse sources. This centralization streamlines troubleshooting, making it easier to pinpoint the root causes of service disruptions. Look no further than Elastic, Datadog, and Splunk, who have leveraged this value proposition for huge success.</p>\n<p><strong>With users increasingly price-sensitive, the <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/blog/breaking-free-from-rising-observability-costs-with-open-cost-efficient-architectures\">high and unpredictable cost of out-of-the-box offerings</a> forces many to resort to aggressive log sampling, a compromise that<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/managing-petabyte-scale-logs-without-sampling\">an effective log management strategy can help avoid</a>.</strong></p>\n<p>In this post, we introduce the CGW Stack (ClickHouse, Grafana, WarpStream or “Can’t Go Wrong”) and demonstrate how this offers compression rates over 10x for typical log data, when coupled with separation of storage and compute, allows a ClickHouse Cloud <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/pricing\">Development tier instance</a> to comfortably host over 1.5TiB of log data (around 5 billion rows assuming a similar number of columns to our sample data): compressing this down to under 100GiB. A fully parallelized query execution engine, coupled with low-level optimizations, ensures query performance remains under 1s for most typical SRE queries on these volumes as demonstrated by our benchmark.</p>\n<p>With compression rates of up to 14x in our benchmarks, this cluster (which allows up to 1TiB of compressed data) can potentially store up to 14 TiB of uncompressed log data. Users can either use this spare capacity to increase storage density even further if query performance is less critical or, alternatively, just use it to simply retain data for longer. We show the savings are dramatic in comparison to other solutions, such as Elastic and <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/datadog-alternatives/\">Datadog</a>, which are between 23x and 42x more expensive for the same data volume.</p>\n<p>While ClickHouse can be used standalone as a log storage engine, we appreciate users often prefer an architecture in which data is first buffered in Kafka prior to its insertion into ClickHouse. This provides a number of benefits, principally the ability to absorb spikes in traffic whilst keeping the insertion load on ClickHouse constant and allowing data to be forwarded to other downstream systems. Looking to keep costs and operations as low as possible in our proposed architecture, we consider a Kafka implementation that also separates storage and compute: WarpStream. We combine this with our standard recommendations of OTEL for log collection and Grafana for visualization—key components for <strong><a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/mastering-kubernetes-observability-guide\">mastering Kubernetes observability</a></strong>.</p>\n<h2 id=\"why-clickhouse\"></h2>\n<p>As a high-performance OLAP database, ClickHouse is used for many use cases, including real-time analytics for<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/blog/working-with-time-series-data-and-functions-ClickHouse\"> time series</a> data. Its diversity of use cases has helped drive a huge range of<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/docs/en/sql-reference/functions/\"> analytical functions</a>, which assist in querying most data types. These query features and very high compression rates, resulting from the column-oriented design and customizable codecs,  have increasingly led users to choose ClickHouse as a log data store. By separating storage and compute, and utilizing object storage as its principal data store, ClickHouse Cloud further increases this cost efficiency, a key architectural choice for teams looking to <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/observability-tco-cost-reduction\">regain control of their observability TCO</a>.</p>\n<p>While we have historically seen<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://blog.cloudflare.com/tag/clickhouse/\"> large CDN providers using ClickHouse for storage of logs</a>, with the cost of logging solutions increasingly in the spotlight, we now increasingly see small and medium-sized businesses considering ClickHouse as a log storage alternative as well.</p>\n<p><span><img alt=\"CGW_stack.png\" src=\"https://clickhouse.com/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2FCGW_stack_858b4d49a5.png&w=2048&q=75\"></span></p>\n<h2 id=\"what-is-warpstream\"></h2>\n<p>WarpStream is an Apache Kafka® protocol compatible data streaming platform that runs directly on top of any commodity object-store. Like ClickHouse, WarpStream was designed from the ground up for analytical use cases and to help manage huge streams of machine-generated data as cost-effectively as possible.</p>\n<p>Instead of Kafka brokers, WarpStream has “Agents”. Agents are stateless Go binaries (no JVM!) that speak the Kafka protocol, but unlike a traditional Kafka broker, any WarpStream Agent can act as the “leader” for any topic, commit offsets for any consumer group, or act as the coordinator for the cluster. No Agent is special, so auto-scaling them based on CPU usage or network bandwidth is trivial.</p>\n<p>This is made possible by the fact that WarpStream separates both compute from storage and_ data from metadata.The_ metadata for every WarpStream “Virtual Cluster” is stored in a custom metadata database that was written from the ground up to solve this exact problem in the most performant and cost-effective way possible.</p>\n<p><span><img alt=\"warpstream_architecture.png\" src=\"https://clickhouse.com/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Fwarpstream_architecture_67cd971aba.png&w=2048&q=75\"></span></p>\n<p>The fact that WarpStream uses object storage as the primary and only storage, with zero local disks, makes it excel with analytical data compared to other Apache Kafka implementations.</p>\n<p>Specifically, WarpStream <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://www.warpstream.com/blog/kafka-is-dead-long-live-kafka\">uses object storage as the storage layer and the network layer to avoid inter-az bandwidth costs entirely</a>, whereas these fees can easily represent 80% of the total cost of a workload with traditional Apache Kafka implementations. In addition, the stateless nature of the Agents makes it practical (even easy) to scale workloads up to GiB/s of data.</p>\n<p>These properties make WarpStream ideal for a logging solution where throughput volumes are often significant and price sensitivity is critical, and delivery latencies of a few seconds are more than acceptable.</p>\n<p>For users looking to deploy a WarpStream for testing, you can follow <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://github.com/warpstreamlabs/warpstream-fly-io-template\">these instructions</a> to deploy a test cluster to Fly.io or <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://railway.app/template/30Xa3Y?referralCode=kKBYG0\">these instructions</a> to deploy on Railway.</p>\n<h2 id=\"clickhouse-cloud-development-tier\"></h2>\n<p>ClickHouse Cloud allows users to deploy ClickHouse with a separation of storage and compute.
