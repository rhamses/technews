The system worked well for what Navaneet calls “small-scale BI workloads,” handling datasets of up to 200 million records, but that was about as far as it could go.</p>\n<p><span><img alt=\"increff.png\" src=\"https://clickhouse.com/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Fincerif_4a1bacf290.png&w=2048&q=75\"></span>\n<em>Increff’s old BI architecture was functional but struggled with growing datasets and scalability.</em></p>\n<p>Things took a turn when Increff onboarded a major fashion retailer with data needs five times larger than anything the platform had seen before. Their search for a better BI solution began.</p>\n<h2 id=\"rebuilding-with-clickhouse\"></h2>\n<p>As Navaneet explains, he and the Increff team had been \"following ClickHouse for a while,\" and this was the perfect opportunity to put it to the test. A two-week proof of concept was all it took to prove its value. <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/cloud\">ClickHouse Cloud</a> efficiently handled their largest datasets with sub-second query latencies. \"It was a successful implementation,\" Navaneet says.</p>\n<p>Confident in ClickHouse’s capabilities, the team began rearchitecting their BI platform. They replaced most of their Pandas-based data transformations with DuckDB, implemented managed ClickHouse instances for warehousing, and added Redis caching to speed up dashboard responsiveness. The revamped system was designed to scale, and it delivered.</p>\n<p><span><img alt=\"increff_v2.png\" src=\"https://clickhouse.com/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Fincreff_v2_f40989caf1.png&w=2048&q=75\"></span></p>\n<p><em>Increff’s old BI architecture was functional but struggled with growing datasets and scalability.</em></p>\n<p>\"With ClickHouse Cloud, we’re handling around a billion records, and we’ve maintained the same performance benchmarks we achieved with smaller datasets,\" Navaneet says. \"We’ve also been able to reduce data preparation times by 70 to 80%.\"</p>\n<h2 id=\"taking-it-up-a-notch\"></h2>\n<p>Since implementing the new architecture, Navaneet and the team have been busy fine-tuning the BI system to push efficiency and performance even further.</p>\n<p>First, they revamped their data modeling, adopting a star schema with a fact constellation design. By creating aggregated fact tables for daily, weekly, and monthly data, they’ve lightened the server load while keeping the granularity needed for analysis. This helps optimize storage and ensures queries run consistently, no matter the scale.</p>\n<p>Next up was partitioning and primary keys. Navaneet and the team analyzed their query patterns and set up partitions using time dimensions like days or weeks —&#160;columns they knew would get frequent use. This keeps data distribution balanced and avoids the pitfalls of skewed partitions, maximizing the efficiency of ClickHouse’s columnar storage.</p>\n<p>Data types played an important role as well. <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/docs/en/sql-reference/data-types/lowcardinality\">LowCardinality</a> types were used for categorical data to improve compression and speed up queries, while unsigned integers provided small but meaningful performance boosts for columns with only positive values.</p>\n<p>Finally, Navaneet and the team have introduced several query optimizations, including using Jinja templating in Superset to make queries more flexible and strip out unnecessary joins. They also explicitly applied ClickHouse’s <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://clickhouse.com/docs/en/sql-reference/statements/select/prewhere\">PREWHERE clause</a> where execution plans needed fine-tuning, shaving precious time off query execution.</p>\n<h2 id=\"the-bi-of-tomorrow\"></h2>\n<p>With the new BI system humming along and improving every day, the Increff team is already looking ahead.
