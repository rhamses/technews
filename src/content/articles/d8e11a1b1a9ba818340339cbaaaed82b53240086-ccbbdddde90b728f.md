Their production environment consisted of 15 Elasticsearch nodes with 168 TB of EBS storage, handling log ingestion from their flagship Alight Worklife system and supporting applications. The infrastructure required an Elastic Platinum subscription, though the team’s operational bandwidth was fully consumed by maintenance, leaving limited capacity to adopt advanced features included in the license.</p> \n<p>The operational pain points included:</p> \n<ul> \n <li><strong>Security vulnerability patching</strong> required working over Christmas holidays to address critical fixes, with no flexibility on timing.</li> \n <li><strong>Elastic upgrades</strong> were time-consuming and required depth of knowledge to manage at scale.</li> \n <li><strong>Logstash</strong> using TCP-socket shipping was unreliable, experiencing log loss at high volumes.</li> \n <li><strong>Backpressure</strong> from Logstash caused two P1 incidents over two years, where the logging subsystem directly impacted microservice tasks.</li> \n <li><strong>Elasticsearch 7.x approaching end of support</strong> created urgency to act before the next Annual Enrollment period (September through January).</li> \n</ul> \n<p>Alight was spending more than $100,000 per month on self-managed infrastructure and Elastic licensing across all environments. All operational budget was consumed by cluster maintenance, leaving zero capacity for innovation.</p> \n<h2 id=\"evaluating-alternatives\">Evaluating alternatives</h2> \n<p>Alight evaluated several alternatives before selecting OpenSearch Service:</p> \n<ul> \n <li><strong>New Relic and Dynatrace</strong> were evaluated for log aggregation but proved prohibitively expensive at Alight’s volume.</li> \n <li><a href=\"https://aws.amazon.com/cloudwatch/\" target=\"_blank\" rel=\"noopener\">Amazon CloudWatch</a> was evaluated but did not meet requirements for complex log research at their volume and visualization complexity.</li> \n</ul> \n<p>Amazon OpenSearch Service is a managed service that makes it straightforward to deploy, operate, and scale OpenSearch clusters in the AWS Cloud. You can use it for use cases such as log analytics and real-time application monitoring. It provisions cluster resources, automatically detects and replaces failed nodes, and scales with a single API call or a few clicks, reducing the operational overhead associated with self-managed infrastructure. It won the evaluation based on five factors:</p> \n<ol type=\"1\"> \n <li><strong>Cost:</strong> significantly cheaper than self-managed Elastic Stack and competing solutions.</li> \n <li><strong>Minimal change management:</strong> as a fork of Elasticsearch 7.10, engineers were already familiar with the query syntax and dashboards.</li> \n <li><strong>Compliance:</strong> using a native AWS service avoided hundreds of hours of vendor compliance, audit, and regulatory work. The team spent a few hours getting approval compared to potentially weeks for an external vendor.</li> \n <li><strong>Cloud-native strategy:</strong> aligned with Alight’s overarching strategy to use cloud-native services.</li> \n <li><strong>Security and data privacy:</strong> keeping everything within their <a The security model uses role separation with least privilege:</p> \n<ul> \n <li><strong>OSIS Ingest Role:</strong> write-only access to OSIS pipelines, assumed by application account roles via cross-account trust.</li> \n <li><strong>OSIS Sink Role:</strong> used by OSIS to write into the OpenSearch domain, with full index access scoped to the ingestion pipeline.</li> \n <li><strong>Security groups:</strong> restrict OSIS traffic to known CIDRs and VPCs.</li> \n</ul> \n<p>Each application has its own indices, and access is governed by application-specific roles.</p> \n<h3 id=\"persistent-buffering\">Persistent buffering</h3> \n<p><a href=\"https://aws.amazon.com/efs/\" target=\"_blank\" rel=\"noopener\">Amazon Elastic File System</a> (Amazon EFS) provides persistent filesystem buffering for the Fluent Bit sidecar, helping prevent log loss during transient failures or backpressure events. In this post, we share how Alight Solutions migrated from self-managed Elasticsearch to Amazon OpenSearch Service. The migration achieved a 55% cost reduction, alleviated approximately 2,000 hours per year of operational overhead, and gave Alight access to advanced observability features they could not prioritize before.
