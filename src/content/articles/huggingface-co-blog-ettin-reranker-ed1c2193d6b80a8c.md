href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#what-is-a-reranker-and-why-pair-one-with-an-embedder\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tWhat is a reranker, and why pair one with an embedder?\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>A reranker (a.k.a. pointwise cross-encoder) is a neural model that takes a <code>(query, document)</code> pair and outputs a single relevance score. Unlike an embedding model, which encodes the query and document separately and computes their similarity from the two embedding vectors, a reranker lets the two texts attend to each other through every transformer layer. That joint encoding is more accurate but also more expensive: the model has to be run once per <code>(query, document)</code> pair rather than once per text.</p>\n<p>Because cross-encoders are too expensive to run over a full corpus, the common production pattern is <strong>retrieve-then-rerank</strong>: a fast embedding model retrieves the top-K candidates (cheap), then a cross-encoder re-orders just those K with high accuracy. The total cost stays bounded while the final ranking is much closer to what an exhaustive cross-encoder pass would produce.</p>\n<p><a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/huggingface/documentation-images/resolve/main/blog/train-reranker/embedding_vs_reranker_model.png\"><img src=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/huggingface/documentation-images/resolve/main/blog/train-reranker/embedding_vs_reranker_model.png\" alt=\"Embedding vs Reranker Models\"></a></p>\n<blockquote>\n<p>Throughout this blogpost I'll use \"reranker\" and \"cross-encoder\" interchangeably.</p>\n</blockquote>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#usage\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tUsage\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>The released models are normal Sentence Transformers <code>CrossEncoder</code> models, so you can use them with just 3 lines of code:</p>\n<pre><code><span>from</span> sentence_transformers <span>import</span> CrossEncoder\nmodel = CrossEncoder(<span>\"cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-32m-v1\"</span>)\nscores = model.predict([\n    (<span>\"Where was Apple founded?\"</span>, <span>\"Apple Inc. was founded in Cupertino, California in 1976 by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne.\"</span>),\n    (<span>\"Where was Apple founded?\"</span>, <span>\"The Fuji apple is an apple cultivar developed in the late 1930s and brought to market in 1962.\"</span>),\n])\n<span>print</span>(scores)\n<span># [11.393298  2.968891]   &lt;- larger means more relevant</span>\n</code></pre>\n<p>For a query and a list of candidates, you can also use <code>rank</code> to get back sorted indices and scores:</p>\n<pre><code>ranked = model.rank(\n    query=<span>\"Which planet is known as the Red Planet?\"</span>,\n    documents=[\n        <span>\"Venus is often called Earth's twin because of its similar size and proximity.\"</span>,\n        <span>\"Mars, known for its reddish appearance, is often referred to as the Red Planet.\"</span>,\n        <span>\"Jupiter, the largest planet in our solar system, has a prominent red spot.\"</span>,\n        <span>\"Saturn, famous for its rings, is sometimes mistaken for the Red Planet.\"</span>,\n    ],\n    top_k=<span>4</span>,\n    return_documents=<span>True</span>,\n)\n<span>for</span> r <span>in</span> ranked:\n    <span>print</span>(<span>f\"(<span>{r[<span>'score'</span>]:<span>.2</span>f}</span>): <span>{r[<span>'text'</span>]}</span>\"</span>)\n<span># (10.82): Mars, known for its reddish appearance, is often referred to as the Red Planet.</span>\n<span># (9.86): Saturn, famous for its rings, is sometimes mistaken for the Red Planet.</span>\n<span># (8.55): Jupiter, the largest planet in our solar system, has a prominent red spot.</span>\n<span># (6.21): Venus is often called Earth's twin because of its similar size and proximity.</span>\n</code></pre>\n<p>You can swap <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-32m-v1\"><code>cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-32m-v1</code></a> for any other size to trade quality for speed. All six accept up to 8K tokens of context (useful for long-document reranking) thanks to ModernBERT's long-context pre-training.</p>\n<p>It is recommended to install <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://github.com/huggingface/kernels\"><code>kernels</code></a> and set <code>model_kwargs={\"dtype\": \"bfloat16\", \"attn_implementation\": \"flash_attention_2\"}</code> for the highest throughput. See the <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#speed\">Speed</a> section below for more details, but in general you can expect a 1.7x-8.3x speedup over default loading depending on model size and sequence length.</p>\n<pre><code><span>from</span> sentence_transformers <span>import</span> CrossEncoder\nmodel = CrossEncoder(\n    <span>\"cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-32m-v1\"</span>,\n    model_kwargs={<span>\"dtype\"</span>: <span>\"bfloat16\"</span>, <span>\"attn_implementation\"</span>: <span>\"flash_attention_2\"</span>},\n)\n</code></pre>\n<h3>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#end-to-end-retrieve-then-rerank-pipeline\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tEnd-to-end retrieve-then-rerank pipeline\n\t</span>\n</h3>\n<p>A complete example with a fast embedder for retrieval and the reranker for the final ordering:</p>\n<pre><code><span>from</span> sentence_transformers <span>import</span> SentenceTransformer, CrossEncoder\n<span># Fast retrieval with a static embedder (sub-millisecond on CPU per query)</span>\nembedder = SentenceTransformer(<span>\"sentence-transformers/static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1\"</span>)\nreranker = CrossEncoder(<span>\"cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-68m-v1\"</span>)\ncorpus = [\n    <span>\"Apple Inc. was founded in Cupertino, California in 1976 by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne.\"</span>,\n    <span>\"The Fuji apple is an apple cultivar developed in the late 1930s.\"</span>,\n    <span>\"Steve Jobs introduced the iPhone in 2007 at Macworld.\"</span>,\n    <span>\"Macintosh computers were sold by Apple from 1984 onward.\"</span>,\n    <span># ... thousands or millions more in production</span>\n]\nquery = <span>\"Where was Apple founded?\"</span>\n<span># Step 1: encode + retrieve top-100</span>\nquery_emb = embedder.encode_query(query, convert_to_tensor=<span>True</span>)\ncorpus_emb = embedder.encode_document(corpus, convert_to_tensor=<span>True</span>)\nscores = embedder.similarity(query_emb, corpus_emb)[<span>0</span>]\ntop_k_idx = scores.topk(<span>min</span>(<span>100</span>, <span>len</span>(corpus))).indices.tolist()\n<span># Step 2: rerank</span>\ntop_k_docs = [corpus[i] <span>for</span> i <span>in</span> top_k_idx]\nranked = reranker.rank(query, top_k_docs, top_k=<span>5</span>, return_documents=<span>True</span>)\n<span>for</span> r <span>in</span> ranked:\n    <span>print</span>(<span>f\"(<span>{r[<span>'score'</span>]:<span>.2</span>f}</span>): <span>{r[<span>'text'</span>]}</span>\"</span>)\n<span># (11.63): Apple Inc. was founded in Cupertino, California in 1976 by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne.</span>\n<span># (4.71): Steve Jobs introduced the iPhone in 2007 at Macworld.</span>\n<span># (1.96): The Fuji apple is an apple cultivar developed in the late 1930s.</span>\n<span># (1.49): Macintosh computers were sold by Apple from 1984 onward.</span>\n</code></pre>\n<p>This is the same shape used by most modern search systems. The retriever decides what enters the funnel, the reranker decides what wins.</p>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#architecture-details\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tArchitecture Details\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>All six rerankers share the same architecture and differ only in their backbone size. The backbone is one of the six <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin\">Ettin encoders</a> from Johns Hopkins University's Ettin suite. These are ModernBERT-style models with unpadded attention, RoPE positional encodings, GeGLU, and 2T tokens of open-license pre-training, supporting up to 8192 tokens of context.</p>\n<p>On top of each encoder, the reranker uses a 4-module classification head that mirrors <code>ModernBertForSequenceClassification</code> but is built from Sentence Transformers' modular components. The underlying <code>Transformer</code> is a plain <code>AutoModel</code> rather than <code>AutoModelForSequenceClassification</code>, which lets us use sequence unpadding for variable-length inputs for Flash Attention 2. At medium-document sequence lengths this is a 1.7x-8.3x speedup over fp32+SDPA depending on model size (see <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#speed\">Speed</a> for the full benchmark):</p>\n<pre><code>1. Transformer(FA2)\n2. Pooling(cls)\n3. Dense(H, H, bias=False, GELU)\n4. LayerNorm(H)\n5. Dense(H, 1, scores)\n</code></pre>\n<p>In my ablations, CLS pooling outperformed mean pooling. That was a little surprising. ModernBERT uses global attention only every third layer and the other two-thirds use local-window attention that cannot reach CLS from distant positions. Empirically, those few global layers carry enough signal to make CLS the better pooling choice.</p>\n<div>\n\t<table>\n\t\t<thead><tr>\n<th>Model</th>\n<th>Backbone</th>\n<th>Hidden So the <code>bf16+FA2 w. padding</code> column is roughly what you'd measure if you swapped <code>sdpa</code> for <code>flash_attention_2</code> in <code>model_kwargs</code> without changing anything else about the model loader. This is the situation that <code>gte-reranker-modernbert-base</code> and <code>granite-embedding-reranker-english-r2</code> from Table 1 are in.</p>\n<p>Lastly, going from <code>bf16+FA2 w. padding</code> to <code>bf16+FA2 w.o. padding</code> is worth between 1.78x (1B) and 2.45x (68M) of additional throughput, and it also cuts peak memory considerably, allowing for higher batch sizes.</p>\n<p>So my recommendation is simple: enable <code>bf16</code> and FA2 together. The six Ettin rerankers will use unpadded inputs by default, since that's what the modular <code>Transformer</code> module from the <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#architecture-details\">Architecture Details</a> section is set up for. The full snippet is the same as in the <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#usage\">Usage</a> section above:</p>\n<pre><code><span>from</span> sentence_transformers <span>import</span> CrossEncoder\nmodel = CrossEncoder(\n    <span>\"cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-150m-v1\"</span>,\n    model_kwargs={\n        <span>\"dtype\"</span>: <span>\"bfloat16\"</span>,\n        <span>\"attn_implementation\"</span>: <span>\"flash_attention_2\"</span>,  <span># See tip below</span>\n    },\n)\n</code></pre>\n<blockquote>\n<p>Use <code>pip install kernels</code> to install FA2. It ships pre-built kernels for a wide range of GPU architectures, CUDA versions, and operating systems.</p>\n</blockquote>\n<p>One caveat for other CrossEncoders: the full speedup is only available for models built with a modular <code>Transformer</code> like the Ettin rerankers. Applying the same two flags to a CrossEncoder that loads through <code>AutoModelForSequenceClassification</code> lands you in the slower <code>bf16+FA2 w. padding</code> column of Table 2 instead.</p>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#training\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tTraining\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>The training script below started as the output of the new <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://github.com/huggingface/sentence-transformers/tree/main/skills\"><code>train-sentence-transformers</code> Agent Skill</a>, shipped in <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://github.com/huggingface/sentence-transformers/releases/tag/v5.5.0\">Sentence Transformers v5.5.0</a>. If you use an AI coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, ...), you can install the skill and ask it to fine-tune a <code>SentenceTransformer</code>, <code>CrossEncoder</code>, or <code>SparseEncoder</code> model on your data. That's how I started working on the recipe below.</p>\n<p>All six rerankers were trained with the same single-stage recipe. Only the learning rate and the per-device batch size vary per model size. The full training script is ~150 lines and uses one published dataset.</p>\n<p>The recipe converged after a single sweep across model sizes. Each size's learning rate was tuned by a small grid search on a ~15% subset of the final training data, and the resulting LRs transferred cleanly to the full-data runs without re-tuning. No per-size tuning beyond LR was needed.</p>\n<h3>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#distillation-recipe\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tDistillation recipe\n\t</span>\n</h3>\n<p>Most published reranker recipes train on human-labeled relevance triples (a query, one positive document, and optionally hard negatives) with a contrastive, pointwise, pairwise, or listwise loss like <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://sbert.net/docs/package_reference/cross_encoder/losses.html#multiplenegativesrankingloss\"><code>MultipleNegativesRankingLoss</code></a>, <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://sbert.net/docs/package_reference/cross_encoder/losses.html#binarycrossentropyloss\"><code>BinaryCrossEntropyLoss</code></a>, <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://sbert.net/docs/package_reference/cross_encoder/losses.html#ranknetloss\"><code>RankNetLoss</code></a>, or <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://sbert.net/docs/package_reference/cross_encoder/losses.html#lambdaloss\"><code>LambdaLoss</code></a>, respectively. See my earlier <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/train-reranker\">Training and Finetuning Reranker Models with Sentence Transformers</a> blogpost, for example.</p>\n<p>But this approach has a few practical and theoretical drawbacks. First, positives need to be human-labeled, which is expensive and slow to scale across many domains. Second, the model only ever sees a label for the small subset of <code>(query, document)</code> pairs that someone went through. Especially after hard negative mining, you end up with a lot of false negatives, e.g. as shown in <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16967\">Hard Negatives, Hard Lessons</a>. Third, the binary nature of this labeling doesn't match reality, where some documents are simply more relevant than others.</p>\n<p>I took a different route here: pointwise MSE distillation from an existing strong teacher reranker. The setup is simple enough to describe in three lines:</p>\n<ul>\n<li><strong>Teacher</strong>: <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/mixedbread-ai/mxbai-rerank-large-v2\"><code>mixedbread-ai/mxbai-rerank-large-v2</code></a> (1.54B parameters).</li>\n<li><strong>Loss</strong>: <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://sbert.net/docs/package_reference/cross_encoder/losses.html#mseloss\"><code>MSELoss</code></a> on the raw teacher logits (range ~[−12, 22]), i.e. without rescaling.</li>\n<li><strong>Training data</strong>: ~143M <code>(query, document, teacher_score)</code> triples.</li>\n</ul>\n<h3>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin-reranker#dataset\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tDataset\n\t</span>\n</h3>\n<p>I've released the training data as a single Hugging Face dataset, <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-v1-data\"><code>cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-v1-data</code></a>, assembled from two sources. Each source is kept as its own split so the provenance is transparent:</p>\n<ol>\n<li>LightOn pre-training data (<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/lightonai/embeddings-pre-training\"><code>lightonai/embeddings-pre-training</code></a>, non-curated): 32 splits covering broad-domain text similarity signal (MTP, FW-EDU, Reddit, PAQ, S2ORC, Amazon, Wikipedia, MS MARCO, etc.). I limit the number of samples for some of the splits, resulting in ~110M <code>(query, document, similarity)</code> triples in total. </li>\n<li>Rescored retrieval data from <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/lightonai/embeddings-fine-tuning\"><code>lightonai/embeddings-fine-tuning</code></a>: 7 splits (<code>msmarco</code>, <code>hotpotqa</code>, <code>trivia</code>, <code>nq</code>, <code>squadv2</code>, <code>fiqa</code>, <code>fever</code>). The source dataset has up to 2048 candidate documents per query (initially scored with <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/Alibaba-NLP/gte-modernbert-base\"><code>Alibaba-NLP/gte-modernbert-base</code></a>), which I rescored with <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/mixedbread-ai/mxbai-rerank-large-v2\"><code>mixedbread-ai/mxbai-rerank-large-v2</code></a> and uploaded as <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/cross-encoder/lightonai-embeddings-fine-tuning-reranked-v1\"><code>cross-encoder/lightonai-embeddings-fine-tuning-reranked-v1</code></a>. That dataset subsamples each query's 2048 candidates down to 256 using the <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04734\">Jang et al.</a> quantile-anchor recipe (all positives + top-16 hard + ~239 quantile-anchor stratified). For training, I pick 64 of those 256 per query: 32 from the score-sorted head (the positive plus the hardest negatives) and 32 medium-difficulty negatives sampled from a band further down the teacher's ranking. See the <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/datasets/cross-encoder/ettin-reranker-v1-data\">dataset card</a> for the exact rank positions.</li>\n</ol>\n<p>Total: ~143M <code>(query,
