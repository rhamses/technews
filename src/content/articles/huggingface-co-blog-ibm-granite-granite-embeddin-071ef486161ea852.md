At 97 million parameters, it scores <strong>60.3 on Multilingual MTEB Retrieval</strong> across 18 languages — the highest retrieval score we've found for any open multilingual embedding model under 100M parameters. The next-best model in that size class, multilingual-e5-small, scores 50.9 on the same benchmark — a <strong>+9.4 point gap</strong> on a mature benchmark.</p>\n<p>At roughly one-third the size of the 311M full-size model, it retains the majority of its retrieval quality across multilingual, code, and long-document benchmarks — a <strong>+12.2 point gain on MTEB Multilingual Retrieval</strong> over its direct predecessor, driven by a new architecture, better training data, and a novel pruning methodology (more on that below). The full-size <strong>granite-embedding-311m-multilingual-r2</strong> scores <strong>65.2</strong> on the same benchmark, a <strong>+13.0 point gain</strong> over its R1 predecessor.</p>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2#what-changed-from-r1\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tWhat Changed from R1\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>The Granite Embedding Multilingual R1 models were built on XLM-RoBERTa encoders with a 512-token context window. The R2 generation is a ground-up rebuild:</p>\n<p><a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/642ae74b25442667e9bd72ed/jhz201_Ud5zUWfA3k4ITA.png\"><img src=\"https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/642ae74b25442667e9bd72ed/jhz201_Ud5zUWfA3k4ITA.png\" alt=\"Granite Embedding R2 Architecture\"></a></p>\n<p><a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/modernbert\">ModernBERT</a> is a recent encoder architecture that revisits the original BERT design with techniques from the last five years of transformer research. The shift brings several practical benefits: alternating attention lengths reduce computation on long sequences (improves throughput on long sequences significantly), rotary position embeddings allow the 32K context window without the positional interpolation hacks that plague older architectures, and Flash Attention 2.0 support speeds up encoding on modern GPUs.</p>\n<p>The new multilingual tokenizers are worth highlighting. Rather than reusing XLM-RoBERTa's 250K-token vocabulary, we adopted existing tokenizers with strong multilingual and code coverage. The 311M model uses the Gemma 3 tokenizer (262K tokens); the 97M model starts from the GPT-OSS tokenizer and prunes it down to a compact 180K-token vocabulary that preserves broad multilingual coverage while reducing the embedding table's parameter footprint. Tokenizer efficiency matters more than people realize — a 32K-token window sounds impressive until your tokenizer burns half of it encoding a single paragraph of Thai.</p>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2#training-the-full-size-311m-model\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tTraining the Full-Size 311M Model\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>The 311M model is a 22-layer ModernBERT encoder with a 262K-token multilingual vocabulary, trained through a multi-stage pipeline:</p>\n<ol>\n<li><strong>Knowledge distillation</strong>: The model learns from multiple teacher models simultaneously. The teachers are Granite 3.3 Instruct and Mistral v0.2 Instruct decoder models, further finetuned for text embeddings, which transfer retrieval-specific knowledge into the 311M encoder architecture.</li>\n<li><strong>Contrastive fine-tuning</strong>: Standard contrastive training on multilingual retrieval pairs — queries matched with relevant and hard-negative passages across 52 languages and code — sharpens the model's ability to distinguish relevant from irrelevant results.</li>\n<li><strong>Model merging</strong>: After training, we merge checkpoints from different training stages and configurations. This combines the strengths of models optimized for different objectives (e.g., multilingual breadth vs. English depth) into a single set of weights without additional training compute.</li>\n<li><strong>Matryoshka Representation Learning</strong>: The model is trained with Matryoshka objectives so that its 768-dimensional embeddings can be truncated to 512, 384, 256, or 128 dimensions with minimal quality loss (see <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2#matryoshka-embeddings-311m\">Matryoshka Embeddings</a> below).</li>\n</ol>\n<p>The result is a model that scores 65.2 on MTEB Multilingual Retrieval and 56.3 on the overall average — a +14.5 point average gain over its R1 predecessor.</p>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2#building-the-compact-97m-multilingual-model\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tBuilding the compact 97M Multilingual model\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<p>The 97M model is trained through a combination of <strong>vocabulary selection</strong> and <strong>knowledge distillation</strong>:</p>\n<ol>\n<li><strong>Vocabulary selection</strong>: The 262K-token vocabulary is reduced to a purpose-trained 180K-token vocabulary that preserves broad multilingual coverage while cutting the embedding table size substantially.</li>\n<li><strong>Knowledge distillation</strong>: The pruned model is then finetuned using knowledge distillation from multiple teacher models (including a <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-8b\">Granite 4.1 8B</a> and Mistral Instruct decoder-based teacher) and contrastive training to improve retrieval quality.</li>\n</ol>\n<p>This approach transfers retrieval-specific knowledge from multiple strong teachers, while reducing the model parameters without sacrificing language coverage. The result is a highly efficient compact model  — scoring 60.3 on MTEB Multilingual Retrieval vs. 65.2 for the full-size model, while being approximately 3x smaller. </p>\n<h2>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2#benchmark-results\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tBenchmark Results\n\t</span>\n</h2>\n<h3>\n\t<a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2#multilingual-retrieval\">\n\t</a>\n\t<span>\n\t\tMultilingual Retrieval\n\t</span>\n</h3>\n<p>Performance across the main benchmark suite sorted by model size. We measured latency and throughput on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU using 512-token chunks:</p>\n<p><a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/642ae74b25442667e9bd72ed/1ltJtX2knfzUA5BekMQsh.png\"><img src=\"https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/642ae74b25442667e9bd72ed/1ltJtX2knfzUA5BekMQsh.png\" alt=\"Speed vs. Quality: 97M model matches 300M-class quality at 3x the parameter efficiency\" title=\"Speed vs Throughput For Top Multilingual Embedding Models under 500M params\"></a></p>\n<p>The 97M model encodes over 2,500 documents per second — comparable throughput to multilingual-e5-small — while delivering substantially higher retrieval quality. This is useful when storage, memory, or similarity-computation cost is a concern — a 256-dimensional embedding takes one-third the storage of a 768-dimensional one, and cosine similarity is proportionally cheaper to compute.</p>\n<p>Here's how retrieval quality holds up across embedding dimensions:</p>\n<p><a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/642ae74b25442667e9bd72ed/myYhY1YHLY9Vs3l0gyMJB.png\"><img src=\"https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/642ae74b25442667e9bd72ed/myYhY1YHLY9Vs3l0gyMJB.png\" alt=\"Matryoshka embeddings: graceful quality degradation across truncated dimensions\" title=\"Performance Change While Prunning the Embeddings to Fewer Entries\"></a></p>\n<p>The quality loss from dimension reduction is remarkably small. Even at 128 dimensions (a <strong>6x reduction</strong>), the model still scores 63.7 on MTEB Multilingual Retrieval and 62.3 on Code — retaining over <strong>97%</strong> of its full-dimension performance. In practice, this means you can substantially reduce your index size and search latency with minimal impact on result quality.
