If rollouts\nare intentionally stale, generated asynchronously, or produced by a backend\nwhere equivalence to the trainer-side policy is unavailable, then some form of\ncorrection is often the right thing to add.</p>\n<p>The first problem here was inference correctness. After moving to V1, the\nrollout backend returned logprobs and runtime behavior that broke the trainer\nassumption. After inference parity is restored,\nthe next improvement is the usual async/off-policy cleanup:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>keep explicit behavior-policy logprobs from rollout time</li>\n<li>recompute trainer-side old-policy logprobs at optimization time</li>\n<li>separate backend mismatch correction from the policy-update ratio</li>\n<li>track diagnostics like ESS for the correction term alongside aggregate\ntrainer metrics</li>\n</ul>\n<p>The main lesson from this migration is narrower: fix backend correctness first,\nthen add corrections for the mismatch that remains.</p>\n<!----></div>"
