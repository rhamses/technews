contentHtml: "<article><div>\n<p>Supabase Auth ships with over 20 built-in social providers: Google, GitHub, Apple, and more. But what about your company's SAML-to-OIDC bridge? A regional identity provider required for compliance? Your self-hosted GitHub Enterprise? Until now, if your identity provider wasn't on the built-in list, you were stuck.</p>\n<p>Today we're launching <strong>Custom OIDC Providers</strong>, letting you connect any standards-compliant OpenID Connect identity provider to your Supabase project. Once configured, custom providers work just like built-in ones: same sign-in flow, same client libraries, same RLS enforcement.</p>\n<p>Built-in providers cover the most common cases, but not every identity provider fits neatly into that list:</p>\n<ul>\n<li><strong>Regional compliance</strong>: Government or industry-specific providers mandated in certain regions.</li>\n<li><strong>Self-hosted providers</strong>: GitHub Enterprise Server, GitLab self-managed, or any on-premise OAuth2 service.</li>\n<li><strong>Niche providers</strong>: Gaming platforms, healthcare identity networks, or industry-specific SSO systems.</li>\n</ul>\n<p>Last year, we shipped <a target=\"_blank\" href=\"https://supabase.com/blog/oauth2-provider\">OAuth 2.1 server capabilities</a> so your Supabase project can <em>be</em> an identity provider. Custom providers complete the picture from the other direction, now your project can also <em>consume</em> any external identity provider.</p>\n<p>Supply your provider's issuer URL, client credentials, and scopes. Supabase handles the rest. The discovery document, endpoints, and JWKS are resolved automatically from <code>{issuer}/.well-known/openid-configuration</code>.</p>\n<div><p><code><br><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>const { data, error } = await supabase.auth.admin.customProviders.createProvider({</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  provider_type: 'oidc',</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  identifier: 'custom:my-provider',</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  name: 'My Provider',</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  client_id: 'your-client-id',</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  client_secret: 'your-client-secret',</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  issuer: 'https://auth.example.com',</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>  scopes: ['openid', 'profile', 'email'],</span></p></div><div><p><span>_<!-- -->10</span></p><p><span>})</span></p></div><br></code></p></div>\n<p>The <code>openid</code> scope is always included automatically, and ID tokens are verified against the provider's JWKS.</p>\n<p>You can also create providers from the Supabase Dashboard: go to <strong>Authentication &gt; Sign In / Providers</strong>, scroll to the <strong>Custom Providers</strong> section at the bottom, and click <strong>New Provider</strong>.</p>\n<p>Once a custom provider is created and enabled, your users sign in through the same flow they'd use with any built-in provider.
