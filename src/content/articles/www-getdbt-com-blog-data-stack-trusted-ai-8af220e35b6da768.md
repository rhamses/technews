About 16 percent of organizations surveyed have deployed AI agents, according to Gartner. Meanwhile, 80 percent of IT leaders don't believe their data is ready for AI, and over 70 percent worry about governance in a world increasingly run by agents.</p><p>That last number should be higher. Way higher.</p><p>Trusted AI requires trusted data: governed, consistent, and contextual. Without it, every AI initiative follows the same arc: a promising pilot, a scaling problem, a stall. The model isn't to blame. The foundation is.</p><h2 id=\"the-shape-of-the-consumer-has-changed\">The shape of the consumer has changed</h2><p>The data stack was built for people. Every layer, from how we model data to how we serve it, assumes a human is sitting at the end of the pipeline: someone with judgment who can pause when a number looks off and decide whether to trust it.</p><p>That assumption is being overturned. Agents are querying your data continuously, taking action autonomously, and they don't stop to reconcile. They act.</p><p>The analyst who runs 50 queries a day has been joined by agents that might run 50,000. Where a human analyst can tolerate lag and ambiguity, an agent making inventory, pricing, or routing decisions cannot.</p><p>The core issue is <strong>context</strong>. An analyst carries context in their head: they know that \"customer\" in the revenue model means paying subscribers, not trial users, because someone told them that in onboarding.</p><p>Agents don't have that. They take what they're given. What a column means, what's authoritative versus stale, what's a trusted curated model versus a raw staging table: that context now has to live in the data itself, in metadata, in contracts, in governed definitions that any system can read.</p><h2 id=\"why-ai-is-guessing-in-the-dark\">Why AI is guessing in the dark</h2><p>Here's what actually happens when you point a generative AI model at your databases, warehouses, or BI datasets without a governed context layer:</p><ul><li>The AI scans whatever tables and columns it can see.</li><li>It guesses which ones to use based on their names.</li><li>It pulls data straight from the warehouse, mixing raw, staging, and curated tables alongside siloed SQL in views and notebooks, with no reliable way to know which represents the actual source of truth.</li></ul><p>The results are consistent. Without governed context, agents:</p><ul><li>Generate unreliable SQL because it can't identify the right models</li><li>Invent or misapply your metric definitions</li><li>Create governance and trust issues with no clear audit trail</li><li>Drive up costs as unreliable queries burn tokens and compute</li></ul><p>All of this happens because the agent is guessing in the dark.
