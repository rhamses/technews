contentHtml: "<div><p>If you lead a data team or build inside one, the <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"2026 State of Analytics Engineering report\" href=\"https://www.getdbt.com/resources/state-of-analytics-engineering-2026\">2026 State of Analytics Engineering report</a> has a finding worth sitting with.</p><p>Data trust is now the top priority for 83% of data teams, up from 66% a year ago. At the same time, 72% say AI-assisted coding is part of how they work. Only 24% say the same about AI-assisted observability.</p><p>Read those three numbers together. Teams are using AI to produce more data, faster. They are not keeping pace on the systems that test, validate, and govern what AI produces. That gap is what happens when you adopt AI acceleration without investing in AI-level quality control.</p><p>Gartner <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"predicted in early 2025\" href=\"https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk\">predicted in early 2025</a> that through 2026, organizations would abandon 60% of AI projects built on data that wasn't AI-ready. That's tracking. The failure mode is always the same. AI gets deployed on infrastructure built for dashboards, not for reasoning. The code ships fast. The data it produces is inconsistent, untested, and undocumented. Stakeholders stop trusting it. The project stalls.</p><p>That's the trust-speed paradox. AI makes data work faster, and faster data work without governance destroys the trust that made the work worth doing.</p><h2 id=\"why-the-gap-is-worse-than-it-looks\">Why the gap is worse than it looks</h2><p>AI doesn't just speed up good data practices. It amplifies whatever practices you already have.</p><p>A team that runs tests, enforces contracts, and documents its models gets faster and sharper with AI. A team that doesn't gets faster at shipping undocumented, untested data. A distribution shift that used to cause a minor dashboard error becomes a hallucination once an agent is reasoning over the data. A schema change that used to break one report now breaks an entire agentic workflow, silently.</p><p>The data debt you've carried for a decade turns into a different kind of liability the moment an agent has to trust the answers it's getting. You can't govern your way out of that after the fact. The governance has to live in the infrastructure before the AI runs on top of it.</p><h2 id=\"what-a-governed-ai-workflow-actually-looks-like\">What a governed AI workflow actually looks like</h2><p>For practitioners, this isn't a new process. It's the dbt workflow you already know, applied consistently, before you add AI acceleration.</p><p>Tests before merges. Every model gets <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"[object Object],, ,[object Object],, and ,[object Object], tests\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/data-tests\"><code>not_null</code>, <code>unique</code>, and <code>accepted_values</code> tests</a> at a minimum. <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"Model contracts\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/mesh/govern/model-contracts\">Model contracts</a> are enforced on your critical models. Breaking changes get caught in CI, not by someone eyeballing a dashboard after the fact.</p><p>Contracts at ingestion. Source freshness checks run. Sources and exposures have owners. When something shifts upstream, your dbt project knows, and the failure surfaces where it should.</p><p>The semantic layer as the source of truth. Your <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"MetricFlow\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/about-metricflow\">MetricFlow</a> metric definitions live in code, not in a BI tool's proprietary layer. A natural language query from an agent returns the same number your CFO uses. The definition is version-controlled, auditable, and consistent everywhere it's queried. That's the whole point of the <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"dbt Semantic Layer\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/use-dbt-semantic-layer/dbt-sl\">dbt Semantic Layer</a>.</p><p>None of this is a new architecture. It's the disciplined use of what dbt was built for, extended to cover the failure modes AI introduces.</p><h2 id=\"five-investments-that-close-the-trust-speed-gap\">Five investments that close the trust-speed gap</h2><p>If you're a data leader staring at the gap between how fast your team adopts AI and how ready your governance is, the path forward is a sequence of investments, not a transformation program.</p><p><strong>Enforce contracts on your most important models.</strong> Not every model needs one today. Start with the models feeding revenue metrics, customer-facing dashboards, and any AI pipeline. <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"Contracts\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/mesh/govern/model-contracts\">Contracts</a> catch breaking changes automatically, and they document the interface other teams depend on.</p><p><strong>Turn on column-level lineage.</strong> If you can't trace a number back to its source, you can't debug an agent that returns the wrong answer. <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"Column-level lineage\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/explore/column-level-lineage\">Column-level lineage</a> gives you that trace automatically in dbt.</p><p><strong>Define your core metrics in MetricFlow.</strong> Revenue, active users, churn. If those definitions live in a BI tool's proprietary layer, an agent can't reach them in a governed way. Moving them into the <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"dbt Semantic Layer\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/use-dbt-semantic-layer/dbt-sl\">dbt Semantic Layer</a> makes them machine-readable and consistent.</p><p><strong>Add AI-assisted observability to match your AI-assisted coding.</strong> The 72% to 24% gap is the most fixable part of this. If your team uses AI to write models, invest the same way in monitoring what those models produce.</p><p><strong>Assign ownership before something breaks.</strong> Ownership is cheap to set and expensive to reconstruct after an incident. Give every source and exposure a named owner. It's the governance investment that pays back fastest when an AI-generated model breaks and nobody can diagnose it.</p><h2 id=\"why-dbt-is-both-the-accelerator-and-the-guardrail\">Why dbt is both the accelerator and the guardrail</h2><p>Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"told CNBC in February 2026\" href=\"https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/09/under-the-hood-of-the-ai-economy-with-databricks-ceo-ali-ghodsi.html\">told CNBC in February 2026</a> that over 80% of the databases on Databricks' platform are now built by AI agents. AI is building data infrastructure faster than people can. </p><p>dbt sits in an unusual spot here. It's the tool that makes AI-assisted development faster, through <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"dbt Wizard\" href=\"https://www.getdbt.com/product/dbt-wizard\">dbt Wizard</a> and the <a target=\"_blank\" title=\"dbt MCP server\" href=\"https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/introducing-dbt-mcp-server\">dbt MCP server</a> that ground agents in your dbt context. It's also the tool that makes AI-generated data trustworthy, through tests, contracts, column-level lineage, and a semantic layer that grounds agent queries in governed definitions.</p><p>Most tools in the stack do one or the other. dbt does both. It's a function of what dbt stores: semantic context in code, version-controlled and machine-readable, at the layer where AI needs to understand what the data means.</p><p>The teams that close this gap first will be the ones that build the foundation before AI runs on top of it.</p></div>"
